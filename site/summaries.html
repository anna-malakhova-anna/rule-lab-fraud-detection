Case summaries

Two or three lines per case, every claim traced to the case

Examples | The guardrail | What it can't catch

When a case opens, the analyst gets a short summary: who the business is, what it is queued for, and the evidence behind its strongest alerts. Every line ends with the case fields it rests on (shown in grey). A check rejects any line that cites nothing, cites a field the case doesn't have, names the wrong rule, or states a number or date its fields don't hold. Summaries here come from a fixed template: no language model and no API key, so the project builds offline. A model can be swapped in later behind the same check.

What it can't catch

The check proves that what a line says can be traced to the case, not that it means what the case means. A made-up sentence with no numbers that cites a real field passes, and two cited numbers swapped into each other's places still match. Before release, the check did catch template lines describing applicants as "open -4 days", and it surfaced a data bug: some accounts had activity before they opened. Both were fixed.

How sure are we? What flatters these numbers, and what they leave out.
